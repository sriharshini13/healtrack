When to Visit the ER?

Come to the Emergency Department for urgent or potentially life-threatening conditions like:

  • Chest pain or pressure
  • Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
  • Signs of stroke (FAST: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 911)
  • Uncontrolled bleeding
  • Sudden, severe headache or dizziness
  • Severe abdominal pain
  • High fever with confusion or stiff neck
  • Obvious broken bones or major injuries
  • Sudden loss of vision
  • Poisoning or overdose

For less severe conditions (like minor cuts, colds, mild fevers), consider contacting your primary physician or visiting an Urgent Care center if appropriate.

Basic First Aid Tips

Disclaimer: This is basic guidance only for minor issues. It is NOT a substitute for professional medical evaluation or treatment. Seek immediate medical attention for anything serious or if you are unsure.

  • Minor Cuts: Clean gently with soap and water, apply direct pressure with a clean cloth to control bleeding, cover with a sterile bandage once bleeding stops.
  • Minor Burns (Superficial): Cool the burn under cool (not cold) running water for 10-15 minutes. Do not use ice, butter, or ointments on fresh burns. Cover loosely with a sterile, non-stick dressing.
  • Sprains: Remember R.I.C.E. - Rest the injured area, apply Ice (cold pack wrapped in a thin towel) for 15-20 minutes every 2-3 hours, use Compression (elastic bandage, snug but not cutting off circulation), and Elevate the injured limb above heart level.
